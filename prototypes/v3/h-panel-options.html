What changes

  • Clicking a shelf swaps the panel to one job: turning that shelf. Everything else waits behind "All settings".
  • The direction buttons become a top-view compass, so "Left" and "Back" match what you see.
  • A single numbered strip replaces the dropdown, counter and Previous/Next buttons.
  • The keyboard tip moves under the compass and no longer covers the model.

Trade-offs

  • Simplest to scan; very little competes for attention.
  • You can't change the finish while a shelf is selected. You have to tap Done first.

What changes

  • Shelf direction leaves the panel entirely. A small card pops up next to the selected shelf with four direction buttons and up/down arrows.
  • The panel shrinks to three calm sections, top to bottom: Bookcase, Arrangement, Room. It looks the same whether or not a shelf is selected.
  • Manual tweaks show as a "Custom" chip plus a one-line reset.

Trade-offs

  • Feels the most direct: you edit the shelf you're looking at.
  • The card has to dodge the bookcase in Angled and Side views, so it needs the placement logic you already wrote for the keyboard hint.
  • Harder to fit on narrow or mobile screens.

What changes

  • Settings become three numbered steps. Only one is open at a time, and the closed ones show a one-line summary.
  • "Shelves" puts arrangement presets first and then lists all 11 shelves, top to bottom like the real bookcase, each with a small direction icon.
  • The selected row expands to four direction buttons. Clicking the model or a row selects it, so no dropdown or Previous/Next is needed.

Trade-offs

  • Shows every shelf's direction at a glance, which also helps on the Build tab.
  • The densest of the three. Eleven rows is fine, but it would get long on a taller model.
My pick: B for desktop, with C's shelf list as the fallback on mobile, where a floating card won't fit.
